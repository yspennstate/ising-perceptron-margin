\begin{abstract}
The storage capacity of the Ising perceptron at margin $\kappa$ is
predicted by a replica-symmetric variational problem.  Huang proved
that the predicted threshold $\alpha_\star(\kappa)$ is an upper bound
at every margin where four conditions on
low-dimensional problems hold.  We verify the four conditions in ball
arithmetic at $\kappa=-0.45$, $-0.05$, $0.05$, $0.0995$ and $0.13$, so
the bound holds there unconditionally; at $\kappa=0.05$ the
capacity is at most $\alpha_\star(0.05)\in[0.781073068,0.781074776]$.
The fixed-point, message-passing and local-concavity conditions hold on the strip
$\kappa\in[-0.65,0.19]$.
At $\kappa\ne0$ the fixed point of the replica-symmetric equations
is known only to a box about $8\cdot10^3$ times wider in one coordinate
than at zero margin, and the zero-margin proof of Huang's two-variable
condition fails at that width.  It is restored by computing the
moments of the condition's maximizing profile as functions of the box,
and by writing each difference of log-partition values the proof needs
as a single expectation about that profile's dual point.  Whether
$\alpha_\star(\kappa)$ is also a lower bound at $\kappa\ne0$ is open.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\emph{Keywords:} binary perceptron, storage capacity,
replica-symmetric formula, computer-assisted proof, ball arithmetic.

60G15, 65G30.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Store $m=\alpha N$ random patterns in a linear classifier with binary
weights: for i.i.d.\ standard Gaussian vectors $g_1,\dots,g_m$ in
$\R^N$ and a margin $\kappa\in\R$, ask whether some
$w\in\{-1,+1\}^N$ satisfies
\[
\frac{\langle g_a, w\rangle}{\sqrt N}\;\ge\;\kappa
\qquad\text{for all } a\le m .
\]
Krauth and M\'ezard \cite{krauth1989storage} computed the
replica-symmetric prediction for the largest such $\alpha$ and
conjectured it sharp; at $\kappa=0$ their constant is
$\alpha_\star=0.8330786\dots$, in the margin framework of Gardner
and Derrida \cite{gardnerderrida1988}.  Rigorous bounds at zero margin
go back to Kim and Roche \cite{kimroche1998} and Talagrand
\cite{talagrand1999}, who proved that the capacity is at most
$1-\varepsilon$ for some $\varepsilon>0$; Altschuler and Tikhomirov
\cite{altschulertikhomirov2024} give a complete proof of the bound
$0.847$ outlined by Krauth and M\'ezard.  Three decades later the zero-margin
conjecture was resolved: Ding and Sun \cite{ding2019capacity}
proved the lower bound and Huang \cite{huang2024capacity} the upper
bound.  Both proofs are conditional on numerical conditions
about explicit low-dimensional variational problems, and those
conditions are verified in interval arithmetic in
\cite{shmalo2026capacity}, at $\kappa=0$.

Huang's upper-bound theorem, however, is stated at every margin.  For
each $\kappa$ it bounds the capacity by $\alpha_\star(\kappa)$, the
root of the replica-symmetric free energy along its fixed-point
branch, conditionally on four numerical conditions at that margin.
The first asks that the fixed-point equations have a unique solution,
along which the free energy changes sign.  The second asks that the
associated approximate-message-passing iteration be stable at that
solution.  The third asks that an explicit function of one real
variable take a negative value at some point.  The fourth
asks that a function $\sS_\star$ of two real variables be
nonpositive on the whole plane (Conditions~\ref{cond:fp}--\ref{cond:2var} of
Section~\ref{sec:system}).  Stojnic's fully-lifted random duality
theory~\cite{stojnic2023} predicts the same replica-symmetric values
at every margin by nonrigorous means.  We verify all four conditions
at five nonzero margins.

\begin{mainthm}[Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
For $\kappa\in\{-0.45,\,-0.05,\,0.05,\,0.0995,\,0.13\}$ the four
conditions of Huang's theorem hold, and the threshold
$\alpha_\star(\kappa)$ lies in the interval
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\kappa=-0.45:\ [1.557652103,\,1.557654133], &
\kappa=-0.05:\ [0.889408031,\,0.889409783],\\
\kappa=0.05:\ [0.781073068,\,0.781074776], &
\kappa=0.0995:\ [0.733478513,\,0.733480205],\\
\kappa=0.13:\ [0.705932217,\,0.705933898]. &
\end{array}
\]
For $\alpha$ above that interval, with probability $1-o(1)$ as
$N\to\infty$ no $w\in\{-1,+1\}^N$ satisfies all $\lceil\alpha N\rceil$
constraints.
\end{mainthm}

At $\kappa=0$ the four conditions are verified in
\cite{shmalo2026capacity}.  There the fixed point $(q_0,\psi_0)$ of the replica-symmetric
equations (Section~\ref{sec:system}) is enclosed to width
$8\cdot10^{-10}$ in $q_0$ and $1.2\cdot10^{-8}$ in $\psi_0$, so
the moment point $a^\star=(\psi_0(1-q_0),q_0)$ of the profile
$\tanh\dot H$, where $\dot H\sim N(0,\psi_0)$, which maximizes the
function of Condition~\ref{cond:2var}, can be treated as an exact
point.  With $M=\tanh\dot H$, the proof of Condition~\ref{cond:2var} works in the moment
body $K$, the set of pairs $(\E[\dot H\Lambda],\E[M\Lambda])$ over
functions $\Lambda$ of $\dot H$ with $|\Lambda|\le1$.  It bounds the entropy term
by convex duality through the log-partition function
$\Phi(b)=\E\log2\cosh(b_1\dot H+b_2M)$, at dual points placed by
certified sign tests; these need the signs of differences
$\Phi(\lambda)-\Phi(\lambda')-(\lambda-\lambda')\cdot x$, $x\in K$,
of order $10^{-7}$.

At $\kappa\ne0$ the threshold is enclosed in an interval, of width
$1.7\cdot10^{-6}$ at $\kappa=0.05$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}), a width
set by the enclosure of the function $G$ whose zero defines
$\alpha_\star$ (Section~\ref{sec:system}) at the quadrature depth used
($1.4\cdot10^{-5}$ at the default depth), and the certificates must
hold at every $\alpha$ of it.  They are therefore
run on the hull of the boxes that contain the fixed point at the two
ends of the interval (Section~\ref{sec:slabs}).  At $\kappa=0.05$
this hull has width $1.5\cdot10^{-6}$ in $q_0$ and $9.2\cdot10^{-5}$
in $\psi_0$, about $2\cdot10^3$ and $8\cdot10^3$ times the widths at
$\kappa=0$; of the width in $\psi_0$, $2.4\cdot10^{-5}$ is the motion
of the fixed point across the interval.  On such a box each enclosure
of $\Phi$ has width about $2\cdot10^{-5}$, and a box no wider than
that motion still leaves $5\cdot10^{-6}$, so the sign tests of
\cite{shmalo2026capacity} are not decided and its argument does not
apply as written.  Narrowing the interval does not remove the
difficulty: at each end $\psi_0$ is enclosed as $R(q_0,\alpha)$ by a
Riemann sum of $4000$ cells, to width $6.9\cdot10^{-5}$
(Section~\ref{sec:regionI}), and the sign tests need each enclosure
of $\Phi$ below $10^{-7}$, so both that width and the motion of the
fixed point would have to shrink, by factors of about $150$ and $50$.

Four changes make it apply on such boxes.  First, $a^\star$ is never stored as
a number.  At the fixed point $\nabla\Phi(1,0)=(\psi_0(1-q_0),q_0)$,
and the certificates use this pair and $s_0=\sqrt{1-q_0}$, the
optimal value of the tilt $s$ over which Huang's two-variable function
is minimized (Section~\ref{sec:twovar}), as functions of the box; the identities at
$a^\star$ are needed only at the true parameters, which lie in the
box.  Second, $\Phi$ is written against the point $b_0=(1,0)$ through
the identity
\[
\Phi(\lambda)-\Phi(b_0)-(\lambda-b_0)\cdot\nabla\Phi(b_0)
=\E\bigl[\log\bigl(\cosh\Delta+M\sinh\Delta\bigr)-M\Delta\bigr],
\qquad \Delta=(\lambda_1-1)\dot H+\lambda_2M,
\]
with $\nabla\Phi(b_0)=a^\star$ at the fixed point, so that the width
of the box enters only at second order in
$|\lambda-b_0|$; within distance $0.01$ of $b_0$ the resulting
enclosures are $50$ to $160$ times tighter than differences of
separate enclosures of $\Phi$.  Third, bounds on the inverse Mills
ratio (Lemma~\ref{lem:mills}) keep the second derivatives of the
constraint term finite where the argument of the inverse Mills ratio
is large.  Fourth, the tilt along each ray from $a^\star$ moves with
a slope fixed by the direction alone, one slope for each of $768$
angular sectors, so that second-derivative bounds on successive radial
bands chain along every ray.

With these changes the four certificates of
\cite{shmalo2026capacity} hold at $\kappa=-0.45$, $-0.05$, $0.05$,
$0.0995$ and $0.13$ (Sections~\ref{sec:twovar}
and~\ref{sec:regionI}).  They are Region~II, a sweep of $K$ outside
an exclusion region about $a^\star$; Region~I, concavity along each
ray from $a^\star$ up to a radius that depends on the direction, so
on a star-shaped neighborhood of $a^\star$, the star; stage~2, a
sweep of the exclusion region minus the star; and the star interior,
a certificate that the star lies inside $K$.

Conditions~\ref{cond:fp}--\ref{cond:lc} hold on the whole strip
$\kappa\in[-0.65,0.19]$, on $1680$ contiguous slabs of width
$5\cdot10^{-4}$ (Section~\ref{sec:slabs} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:strip}).  The fixed point is
unique because the derivative of $q\mapsto P(R(q,\alpha))$, the
composition of the two fixed-point equations of
Section~\ref{sec:system}, has a certified bound below $1$: for
$\kappa\in[-0.65,0.10]$ on each slab rectangle, and for
$\kappa\in(0.10,0.19]$ on rectangles padded by $5\cdot10^{-4}$ in $q$
about the fixed points located at the ends of each sub-slab
(Section~\ref{sec:nak}).  On $(0.10,0.19]$ Nakajima's uniqueness
theorem~\cite{nakajima2025}, whose premise is verified in ball
arithmetic, gives uniqueness a second time.  The Hessian at the maximizer $(1,0)$ of the two-variable function
with the tilt held at its optimal value, negative definite at
$\kappa=0$ by \cite[Claim~2.6 and Appendix~B.3]{huang2024capacity},
is negative definite at six margins (Section~\ref{sec:hessian}).

The strip uses the one-term formula for $\partial R/\partial q$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:dR}), where $\psi=R(q,\alpha)$ is the second
fixed-point equation.  Lemma~\ref{lem:stat} is the computation of Ding and
Sun~\cite[Lemma~7.1 and Corollary~7.2]{ding2019capacity} carried out
off the fixed-point branch, and Lemma~\ref{lem:dR} is Nakajima's
formula~\cite[eq.~(18)]{nakajima2025}, rewritten for $R$; Nakajima
states it for $\kappa\ge0$, and the computation holds for every
$\kappa$.

For $\kappa<0$, Section~\ref{sec:lower} gives the lower bound
$\max\{\alpha_\star(0),\alpha^{\mathrm{sym}}_c(|\kappa|)\}$, where
$\alpha^{\mathrm{sym}}_c(r)$ is the threshold of the symmetric binary
perceptron at width $r$, by
monotonicity and by embedding a symmetric perceptron; it lies far
below $\alpha_\star(\kappa)$.  The enclosures of the special functions
are those of~\cite{shmalo2026capacity}; integrals over wide
parameter balls are Riemann sums (Section~\ref{sec:architecture}).

Section~\ref{sec:system} states the replica-symmetric system,
Huang's four conditions and the two identities.
Section~\ref{sec:curve} describes the capacity curve in floating
point.  Section~\ref{sec:slabs} proves Conditions~\ref{cond:fp}--%
\ref{cond:lc} on the strip, and Section~\ref{sec:twovarfn} treats the

\section{The system and the four conditions}\label{sec:system}

Let $\varphi$ and $\Psi$ denote the standard Gaussian density and
upper tail, $E=\varphi/\Psi$ the inverse Mills ratio (an increasing
function; $E'=E\,(E-u)\in(0,1)$, proved with its second-derivative
bounds in Lemma~\ref{lem:mills}), and $Z\sim N(0,1)$.  Fix
$\kappa\in\R$ and write
\[
u(z;q,\kappa)=\frac{\kappa-\sqrt{q}\,z}{\sqrt{1-q}},\qquad 0<q<1 .
\]
Following Ding--Sun and Huang, the replica-symmetric system is
\begin{align}
P(\psi)&=\E\bigl[\tanh^2(\sqrt{\psi}\,Z)\bigr],\qquad
R(q,\alpha)=\frac{\alpha}{1-q}\,\E\bigl[E(u(Z))^2\bigr],
\label{eq:PR}\\
G(\alpha,q,\psi)&=-\frac{\psi(1-q)}{2}
+\E\log\bigl(2\cosh(\sqrt{\psi}\,Z)\bigr)
+\alpha\,\E\log\Psi\bigl(u(Z)\bigr),
\label{eq:G}
\end{align}
with the fixed-point branch $q=P(\psi)$, $\psi=R(q,\alpha)$ and the
threshold $\alpha_\star(\kappa)$ defined as the zero of
$G_\star(\alpha)=G(\alpha,q_\star(\alpha),\psi_\star(\alpha))$; we write
$(q_0,\psi_0)=(q_\star,\psi_\star)(\alpha_\star(\kappa))$.  At
$\kappa=0$, where $u=-\gamma z$ in law with $\gamma=\sqrt{q/(1-q)}$,
this is the zero-margin system of \cite{shmalo2026capacity}.
(Display~(14) of \cite{huang2024capacity} has $-\alpha\,\E\log\Psi$;
the fixed-point equations require the plus sign, which is the sign
in \cite[eq.~(4)]{ding2019capacity}.)

Huang's Theorem~3.6~\cite{huang2024capacity} reads: for any
$\kappa\in\R$, under his Conditions~1.3, 3.1, 3.3 and~3.4, the
probability that some $w\in\{-1,+1\}^N$ satisfies the first
$\lceil\alpha N\rceil$ constraints tends to $0$ for every
$\alpha>\alpha_\star(\kappa)$.  Each of the four conditions is
defined at every $\kappa$ and depends on it.  Conditions~\ref{cond:fp},
\ref{cond:amp}, \ref{cond:lc} and~\ref{cond:2var} below are his
Conditions~3.1, 3.3, 3.4 and~1.3: Condition~\ref{cond:fp} is his
Condition~3.1 asked on a closed interval, which implies it on the open
one; Condition~\ref{cond:amp} is his Condition~3.3 with
$F'_{1-q_0}$ written through the inverse Mills ratio;
Condition~\ref{cond:lc} is his Condition~3.4 with one witness in place
of the infimum; and Condition~\ref{cond:2var} is his Condition~1.3,
whose function, his display~(8), is written out at general $\kappa$ in
Section~\ref{sec:twovar}.

\begin{cond}[fixed point]\label{cond:fp}
There are an interval $[\alpha_-,\alpha_+]$ and a rectangle
$[\alpha_-,\alpha_+]\times[q_{\mathrm{lb}},q_{\mathrm{ub}}]$ such that
for every $\alpha\in[\alpha_-,\alpha_+]$ the map $q\mapsto
P(R(q,\alpha))$ has a unique fixed point in
$(q_{\mathrm{lb}},q_{\mathrm{ub}})$, with
$\sup_{q\in(q_{\mathrm{lb}},q_{\mathrm{ub}})}(P\circ R_\alpha)'(q)<1$,
and $G_\star(\alpha)=G(\alpha,q_\star(\alpha),\psi_\star(\alpha))$,
with $q_\star(\alpha)$ that fixed point and
$\psi_\star(\alpha)=R(q_\star(\alpha),\alpha)$, is strictly decreasing
on $[\alpha_-,\alpha_+]$ with a unique root there.
\end{cond}

Huang's $\kappa=0$ instance (his Proposition~3.2) takes an interval of
width $10^{-9}$ about $\alpha_\star(0)$.  We call the uniqueness and
contraction part the rectangle clause.

\begin{cond}[approximate message passing, AMP]\label{cond:amp}
At the fixed point,
\[
\alpha\,\E[\sech^4(\sqrt{\psi_0}Z)]\;
\frac{\E[E'(u(Z;q_0,\kappa))^2]}{(1-q_0)^2}\;<\;1.
\]
\end{cond}

\begin{cond}[local concavity]\label{cond:lc}
Let $A=1-q_0$, $F(x)=E\bigl((\kappa-x)/\sqrt A\bigr)/\sqrt A$,
$\hat f_0=-F'/(1+AF')$,
$\mu(\hat z)=\E[(\hat z+\cosh^2(\sqrt{\psi_0}Z))^{-1}]$,
$d_0=\alpha_\star\E[F'(\sqrt{q_0}Z)]$ and
\[
\ell(\hat z)=\hat z-\alpha_\star\,\E\!\left[\frac{\hat f_0(\sqrt{q_0}Z)}
{1+\mu(\hat z)\hat f_0(\sqrt{q_0}Z)}\right]-d_0,\qquad \hat z>-1.
\]
Then $\ell(\hat z)<0$ for some $\hat z>-1$.
\end{cond}

This is \cite[Condition~3.4]{huang2024capacity}, whose function is
called $\lambda$ there; a single witness suffices, since Huang's
condition asks that $\inf_{\hat z>-1}\ell(\hat z)<0$.

\begin{cond}[two-variable function]\label{cond:2var}
$\sS_\star(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\le0$ for all
$(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\in\R^2$, where $\sS_\star$ is the
two-variable function of Section~\ref{sec:twovar}; equality holds at
$(1,0)$.
\end{cond}

\subsection{Two identities}\label{sec:identities}

The function $G$ is that of Ding and Sun~\cite[eq.~(4)]{ding2019capacity},
who define it at general margin; the two lemmas below record its
partial derivatives and the derivative of $R$ at every $\kappa$.

\begin{lemma}[stationarity]\label{lem:stat}
For all $\alpha>0$, $\kappa\in\R$, $\psi>0$ and $0<q<1$,
\[
\frac{\partial G}{\partial\psi}=\frac{q-P(\psi)}{2},
\qquad
\frac{\partial G}{\partial q}=\frac{\psi-R(q,\alpha)}{2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

In particular the critical points of $G$ are exactly the solutions of
Ding and Sun's fixed-point system $q=P(\psi)$, $\psi=R(q,\alpha)$
\cite{ding2019capacity}, at every margin.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:stat}]
The first identity is Gaussian integration by parts on the
$\log\cosh$ term.  For the second, differentiating $u$ in $q$ gives
\[
u_q=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1-q}}
\Bigl(-\frac{z}{\sqrt q}+\frac{u}{\sqrt{1-q}}\Bigr),
\qquad
\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\,\E\log\Psi(u)=-\E\bigl[E(u)\,u_q\bigr].
\]
Stein's lemma applied to the $z$ part,
$\E[zE(u)]=-\sqrt{q/(1-q)}\;\E[E'(u)]$, together with the Mills
identity $E'(u)=E(u)^2-uE(u)$, collapses the two pieces:
\[
\E[E(u)\,u_q]
=\frac{\E[E'(u)]+\E[u\,E(u)]}{2(1-q)}
=\frac{\E[E(u)^2]}{2(1-q)}
=\frac{R(q,\alpha)}{2\alpha}.
\]
Hence $\partial_q G=\psi/2-\alpha\cdot R/(2\alpha)=(\psi-R)/2$.
\end{proof}

Both derivatives vanish exactly on the fixed-point branch.  On a
rectangle carrying the contraction of Condition~\ref{cond:fp} the
branch $\alpha\mapsto(q_\star,\psi_\star)$ is $C^1$ (the implicit
function theorem applies: $1-(P\circ R_\alpha)'$ is bounded away
from zero), and the envelope theorem gives
$G_\star'(\alpha)=\E\log\Psi(u)<0$ strictly ($\Psi<1$ pointwise), for
every $\kappa$: the root $\alpha_\star(\kappa)$ is unique on the
branch.  This is the
general-margin form of the monotonicity clause in
Condition~\ref{cond:fp}, and it is also what turns the boxes in which
Section~\ref{sec:slabs} locates the fixed point (located boxes) into per-slab
$\alpha_\star$ intervals: a certified sign of $G$ at a located box is
a certified side of $\alpha_\star$.  The branch is increasing in
$\alpha$: $P$ is increasing, since $\tanh^2(\sqrt\psi\,|z|)$ increases
with $\psi$ for each $z$; $R$ is linear in $\alpha$ and increasing in
$q$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:dR}); so
$q_\star'(\alpha)=P'(\psi_\star)\,(R/\alpha)\big/\bigl(1-(P\circ
R_\alpha)'(q_\star)\bigr)>0$, and $\psi_\star=R(q_\star,\alpha)$
increases with it.  Hence the hull of the boxes located at the two
ends of an $\alpha$ interval contains $(q_\star(\alpha),\psi_\star(\alpha))$
for every $\alpha$ in the interval.

\begin{lemma}[derivative of $R$]\label{lem:dR}
For all $\alpha>0$, $\kappa\in\R$ and $0<q<1$,
\[
\frac{\partial R}{\partial q}
=\frac{\alpha}{(1-q)^2}\,
\E\Bigl[E'(u)^2+2\,E(u)^2E'(u)\Bigr]\;>\;0 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating \eqref{eq:PR} in $q$ produces
$R/(1-q)+\tfrac{\alpha}{1-q}\E[2EE'u_q]$.  Splitting $u_q$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:stat} and applying Stein's lemma to
$\E[z\,EE'(u)]$ (with $(EE')'=E'^2+EE''$ and
$E''=E'(E-u)+EE'-E$) turns the cross term into
\[
\E[2EE'u_q]=\frac{\E[E'^2+2E^2E'-E^2]}{1-q},
\]
and the $-\alpha\E[E^2]/(1-q)^2$ piece cancels $R/(1-q)$ exactly.
Positivity is immediate since $E'\in(0,1)$.  In both proofs
differentiation under the integral sign is dominated
($|z\tanh(\sqrt\psi z)|\le\sqrt\psi\,z^2$, and the $q$-integrands
grow at most quadratically in $|z|$, locally uniformly in the
parameters), and Stein's lemma applies because $E(u(z))$ and
$(EE')(u(z))$ are absolutely continuous with bounded derivative in
$z$.
\end{proof}

With $B(q)=(1-q)\E[E(u)^2]$ one has $R=\alpha B(q)/(1-q)^2$, and
Nakajima's computation of $B'$~\cite[eq.~(18)]{nakajima2025} gives at every margin the monotonicity of $R$ that Ding
and Sun prove at $\kappa=0$~\cite[Lemma~7.3]{ding2019capacity}.  The
one-term form is used twice: the integrand is a product of quantities
with classical bounds, so its interval enclosure is tight enough for
the contraction product on each slab; and positivity makes
$q\mapsto P(R(q))$ increasing, which is what the location step of
Section~\ref{sec:slabs} bisects on.

trivialize.

\section{Certificates on a strip}\label{sec:slabs}

For $\kappa$ in slabs of width $5\cdot10^{-4}$ covering
$[-0.65,0.19]$, with per-slab parameter rectangles seeded by the
continuation, we verify
Conditions~\ref{cond:fp}--\ref{cond:lc} in ball arithmetic (a
rectangle is a product of intervals in $(\alpha,q)$, a box a product
of intervals in $(q_0,\psi_0)$)
(\texttt{python-flint}~0.9.0 on FLINT~3.6.0, 80-bit working precision, every input a ball
covering the whole slab).

\subsection{Numerical architecture}\label{sec:architecture}

The computation rests on three rules.

First, adaptive complex quadrature cannot resolve enclosure width
that comes from parameter balls: subdividing the integration
variable does not shrink it.
So every wide-parameter integral is a real-cell Riemann sum with
exact per-cell Gaussian masses.  Second, the ball evaluation of
$\varphi/\Psi$ fails on wide arguments in the steep region (the
enclosure of $\Psi$ crosses zero), while the ratio is monotone; all
factors built from $\varphi/\Psi$ are therefore enclosed by two thin endpoint
evaluations, and the sign-critical sums use the mean-value cell rule
(midpoint value plus a derivative correction; on a cell of width $h$
its error is $O(h^2)$ against the plain hull's $O(h)$, with constants
set by the derivative enclosures on that cell).  Third, expectations that nearly cancel must be
evaluated as one correlated integrand, never as separate sums whose
parameter widths add.  For Condition~\ref{cond:lc} this is the
identity (with $\tau=E'$, $A=1-q_0$, and $\beta=\mu(\hat z)-A$ for
$\mu(\hat z)=\E[(\hat z+\cosh^2(\sqrt{\psi_0}Z))^{-1}]$)
\[
\ell(\hat z)=\hat z+\alpha_\star\E\!\left[\frac{\beta\,\tau^2}{A(A+\beta\tau)}\right].
\]
Indeed $F'(\sqrt{q_0}Z)=-\tau/A$, so $\hat f_0=\tau/(A(1-\tau))$,
$\hat f_0/(1+\mu\hat f_0)=\tau/(A+\beta\tau)$ and
$d_0=-\alpha_\star\E[\tau]/A$, and
$\tau/A-\tau/(A+\beta\tau)=\beta\tau^2/(A(A+\beta\tau))$.  The
integrand is monotone in $\tau$ for fixed $(\mu,A)$, regular on all
of $\tau\in[0,1]$, and bounded by $|\beta|/(A\mu)$: the map $\tau\mapsto\tau^2/(A+\beta\tau)$
has derivative $\tau(2A+\beta\tau)/(A+\beta\tau)^2$, and $A+\beta\tau$
lies between $A$ and $A+\beta=\mu$, both positive, so
$2A+\beta\tau=A+(A+\beta\tau)>0$; the integrand is therefore monotone
in $\tau$, and its absolute value is at most its value at $\tau=1$,
which is $|\beta|/(A\mu)$.

\subsection{Uniqueness by contraction}

On each of the $1500$ slabs of $[-0.65,0.10]$ the checks are: (i) the contraction product
$\sup_\psi P'\cdot\sup_q|\partial R/\partial q|$, which bounds
$\sup_q(P\circ R_\alpha)'$, is below $1$ on the slab rectangle, via Lemma~\ref{lem:dR}; (ii)
existence: the endpoints of the $q$ interval map strictly inward
under $P\circ R_\alpha$ on each of eight $\alpha$ sub-intervals;
(iii) the crossing: at the two thin $\alpha$ endpoints a certified
Picard iteration locates the fixed point (the invariant is that the
image of any set containing the fixed point contains it) and the sign
of $G$ resolves through a mean-value form that keeps the stationarity
cancellations of Lemma~\ref{lem:stat} explicit,
$G(\alpha_-)>0>G(\alpha_+)$; (iv)
Condition~\ref{cond:amp}; (v) Condition~\ref{cond:lc} at Huang's
witness $\hat z=-0.6693$.  Together with the strict decrease of
$G_\star$, (i)--(iii) discharge Condition~\ref{cond:fp} in full on
the slab: $\alpha_\star(\kappa)$ exists, is unique, and lies in the
slab's $\alpha$ interval for every $\kappa$ in the slab.

The contraction product (i) is below $1$ on each of these $1500$
slabs; on $(0.10,0.19]$ it is not below $1$ on every slab (it

the enclosure of $\partial R/\partial q$ is about twice too wide for

The $1500$ slabs are contiguous, cover $[-0.65,0.10]$, and each

\section{The two-variable function}\label{sec:twovarfn}

\subsection{The maximizer and its Hessian}\label{sec:hessian}

The local ingredient of Condition~\ref{cond:2var} is the negative
definiteness of the Hessian $\mathcal H$ at $(1,0)$ of the two-variable
function of Section~\ref{sec:twovar} with the tilt held at its optimal
value $s_0=\sqrt{1-q_0}$; at $\kappa=0$ this is
\cite[Claim~2.6]{huang2024capacity}, verified in his Appendix~B.3.
The second derivative in the tilt is positive: it is
$\psi_0-\alpha\E[E'\eta^2]$, with $\eta=E(u)/\sqrt{1-q_0}$ and $0<E'<1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:mills}) and
$\alpha\E[\eta^2]=\psi_0$.  Minimizing over the tilt therefore
subtracts a positive semidefinite matrix (a Schur complement), so the Hessian of the tilt-optimized function at
$(1,0)$ is bounded above by $\mathcal H$ in the Loewner order and is
negative definite whenever $\mathcal H$ is.  His second-derivative expansion,
\emph{before} the zero-margin specialization, gives at every $\kappa$
the same quadratic form
\[
\langle \xi, \mathcal H \xi\rangle=
-(I_1\xi_1^2+2I_2\xi_1\xi_2+I_3\xi_2^2)+C_1y_1^2+C_2y_1y_2+C_3y_2^2,
\]
where $y_1=I_1\xi_1+I_2\xi_2$ and $y_2=I_2\xi_1+I_3\xi_2$,
with prior-side integrals $I_j$ unchanged in form
($I_1=\psi_0(1-q_0)-2\psi_0^2(1-4q_0+3p_4)$,
$I_2=\psi_0(1-4q_0+3p_4)$, $I_3=q_0-p_4$, $p_4=\E\tanh^4(\sqrt
{\psi_0}Z)$) and constraint-side constants $C_j$ that at
$\kappa\ne0$ must be taken from the pre-specialization display.  Put
$\hat H=\sqrt{q_0}\,Z$, $u=(\kappa-\hat H)/\sqrt{1-q_0}$,
$\eta=E(u)/\sqrt{1-q_0}$ and $F'=-E'(u)/(1-q_0)$.  His zero-margin
closed forms rest on two identities of the
unshifted law, of which $\alpha\,\E[\eta^2]=\psi_0$ survives at every
margin (it is the same identity behind the unbounded-tilt directions
of Section~\ref{sec:twovar}) while
$\alpha\,\E[\eta \hat H]=-q_0(1-q_0)\psi_0$ does not.  We evaluate the
general-margin $C_j$ directly.  With
$Q=-\hat H/q_0+\bigl(\kappa-\hat H-(1-q_0)\eta \bigr)/(1-q_0)$,
every expectation over the single Gaussian $\hat H$,
\[
C_1=\frac{\alpha}{\psi_0^2}\,\E[F'\eta^2],\qquad
C_2=-\frac{2\alpha}{\psi_0}\,\E[F'\eta Q]+\frac{2}{1-q_0},
\]
\[
C_3=\alpha\,\E[F'Q^2]+\frac{2\alpha}{q_0(1-q_0)}\,\E[\eta \hat H]
-\alpha\Bigl(\frac{3}{(1-q_0)^2}+\frac{1}{q_0(1-q_0)}\Bigr)
\E\bigl[\eta \,(\kappa-\hat H-(1-q_0)\eta)\bigr].
\]
The certified evaluation encloses each expectation by the mean-value
cell rule with explicit quadratic tail majorants ($\eta$ grows at most
linearly on the left tail, $F'$ is bounded by $1/(1-q_0)$ in
magnitude, and every integrand is degree at most two in $|z|$), and
the near-cancelling prior moments are evaluated as single correlated
integrands.  The certified enclosures at the six margins are
one-sided where sign is the claim:
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
\kappa=-0.45: & \mathcal H_{11}\le-0.0647, & \det \mathcal H\ge 2.9\cdot10^{-4},\\
\kappa=-0.05: & \mathcal H_{11}\le-0.0471, & \det \mathcal H\ge 1.6\cdot10^{-4},\\
\kappa=0:     & \mathcal H_{11}\le-0.0451, & \det \mathcal H\ge 1.8\cdot10^{-4},\\
\kappa=0.05:  & \mathcal H_{11}\le-0.0424, & \det \mathcal H\ge 1.2\cdot10^{-4},\\
\kappa=0.0995:& \mathcal H_{11}\le-0.0400, & \det \mathcal H\ge 1.0\cdot10^{-4},\\
\kappa=0.13:  & \mathcal H_{11}\le-0.0385, & \det \mathcal H\ge 8.8\cdot10^{-5}.
\end{array}
\]
The bound on $|\mathcal H_{11}|$ decreases as $\kappa$ increases.

Certification requires parameter boxes far tighter than the slabs
provide (the $C_j$ move with $q$ at rate about $10$, while $\det\mathcal H\sim10^{-4}$), which is
what the deep $\alpha_\star$ intervals above are for.  Iterating
$q\mapsto P(R(q,\alpha))$ over the whole deep $\alpha_\star$ interval gives boxes
that contain the fixed point for every $\alpha$ in it
($3.6\cdot10^{-6}$ in $q_0$, $5.2\cdot10^{-5}$ in $\psi_0$ at
$\kappa=0.05$).  On these boxes the certificate
resolves: $\mathcal H_{11}<0$ and $\det \mathcal H>0$ in ball arithmetic, with the
near-cancellations $1-4q_0+3p_4$ and $q_0-p_4$ evaluated as single
correlated integrands and the five constraint-side integrals by
mean-value Riemann sums with elementary derivatives of $\varphi/\Psi$.
The same chain certifies at $\kappa=0$ on the parameter rectangle
of~\cite[Section~7]{ding2019capacity},
where the $C_j$ enclosures lie inside Huang's certified
intervals, and at $-0.05$, $0.0995$, $-0.45$ (where $C_1=-0.855$,
against $-0.718$ at zero margin) and $0.13$.

Along the continuation $\mathcal H$ stays negative definite at least
up to $\kappa=1.15$ and loses definiteness inside $(1.1812,1.1844)$,
while the Hessian of the tilt-optimized function remains negative
out to at least $\kappa=1.5$, as the Schur relation allows, so past
$\kappa\approx1.18$ a local argument must re-optimize the tilt, as
the ray certificates below already do.

\subsection{The function at general margin}\label{sec:twovar}

Huang's two-variable function at margin $\kappa$, his display~(8)
with $\eta=F(\hat H)$ written for his $F_{1-q_0}(\hat H)$, is
\[
\sS(\Lambda,s)=\frac{s^2\psi_0}{2}+\mathrm{ent}(\Lambda)
+\alpha\,\E\log\Psi\!\left(
\frac{\kappa-(a_2/q_0)\hat H-(a_1/\psi_0)\eta}{D}+s\eta\right),
\]
where $\dot H\sim N(0,\psi_0)$ and $\hat H\sim N(0,q_0)$ are
independent, $M=\tanh\dot H$ (Huang's notation), the profile
$\Lambda$ is a function of $\dot H$ with $|\Lambda|\le1$,
$\mathrm{ent}(\Lambda)=\E\,h\bigl(\tfrac{1+\Lambda}{2}\bigr)$ with
$h(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)$, $\eta=E(u)/\sqrt{1-q_0}$ is the
variable of Section~\ref{sec:hessian} (the margin enters through
$\eta$ and through the additive $\kappa$ in the $\log\Psi$
argument), $a_1=\E[\dot H\Lambda]$, $a_2=\E[M\Lambda]$,
$D=D(a)=\sqrt{1-a_2^2/q_0}$, and
$\sS_\star(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)=\inf_{s\ge0}
\sS(\tanh(\lambda_1\dot H+\lambda_2 M),s)$.
Condition~\ref{cond:2var} asks $\sS_\star\le0$ everywhere.

The moment reduction~\cite[Lemma~5.1]{shmalo2026capacity} holds at
every $\kappa$ with the same proof, because the margin enters only the
constraint term.  The profile enters only through the moment pair
$a(\Lambda)=(\E[\dot H\Lambda],\E[M\Lambda])$, which ranges
over a compact convex body $K$ (the moment body of $(\dot H,M)$), and
on $K$ the entropy term is bounded through
$H(a)=\max\{\mathrm{ent}(\Lambda):\E[\dot H\Lambda]=a_1,\ \E[M\Lambda]=a_2\}$
by convex duality, $H(a)\le\Phi(b)-b\cdot a$ for every dual point $b$, with
$\Phi(b)=\E\log2\cosh(b_1\dot H+b_2M)$ free of $\kappa$.  With
\[
\mathcal F(a,s)=\frac{s^2\psi_0}{2}+H(a)
+\alpha\,\E\log\Psi\!\left(
\frac{\kappa-(a_2/q_0)\hat H-(a_1/\psi_0)\eta}{D(a)}+s\eta\right),
\qquad
\bar\sS(a)=\inf_{s\ge0}\mathcal F(a,s),
\]
the lemma gives
\[
\sS_\star(\lambda)\le\bar\sS\bigl(a(\tanh(\lambda_1\dot H+\lambda_2M))\bigr),
\qquad\lambda\in\R^2 .
\]
The certificates bound $\mathcal F$ with $H(a)$ replaced by
$\Phi(b)-b\cdot a$ at the dual point $b$ of each cell.  Two structural facts organize
the verification.  The maximizing profile $\tanh\dot H$, that is
$\lambda=(1,0)$, has moment point $a^\star=(\psi_0(1-q_0),\,q_0)$ for
every $\kappa$: this is the identity $\nabla\Phi(1,0)=(\E[\dot H M],\E[M^2])
=(\psi_0(1-q_0),q_0)$ (Gaussian integration by parts and the
fixed-point equations), and \cite[Lemma~7.15]{huang2024capacity} gives
$\sS_\star=0$ there with optimal tilt $s_0=\sqrt{1-q_0}$, both in
closed form.  And on a region close to the half-plane $\lambda_1<0$
the $s$-infimum is $-\infty$: $\alpha\,\E[\eta^2]=\psi_0$ exactly
at the fixed point, so the quadratic tilt coefficient cancels, and
wherever the surviving linear coefficient of the tilt is nonpositive
the function runs away (in floating point, the boundary of that region at
$\kappa=0.05$ crosses $\lambda_1=0.04$ on the $\lambda_2=0$ axis and
bends left as $|\lambda_2|$ grows).  Condition~\ref{cond:2var}
takes the infimum over all $s\ge0$, so a negative value at one finite
tilt is a witness, and the certificates of Section~\ref{sec:regionI}
exhibit one cell by cell.

In floating point the function is nearly the same at every $\kappa$
of the curve: a single peak
at $(1,0)$, off-center values below $-3\cdot10^{-3}$, negative far
rays, eigenvalues near $-0.22$ and $-0.0045$ of the Hessian of
$\sS_\star$ in $\lambda$ at $(1,0)$ (finite differences), distance
near $0.017$ from the boundary of $K$, with the weak eigenvalue degrading toward
deep negative margin ($-0.0013$ at $\kappa=-1.5$).

\section{Certifying the two-variable condition}\label{sec:regionI}

The zero-margin certificate has four parts: Region~II, a sweep of
the moment body outside an exclusion region, the exclusion region
being a product of two intervals about $a^\star$ in the moment plane
that contains the star; Region~I, ray certificates on the star, one
for each radial band and angular cell; stage~2, a sweep of the
exclusion region minus the star; and the star interior, a certificate
that the star lies strictly inside the moment body.  At $\kappa\ne0$ all four meet the same difficulty.
At zero margin the parameters $(q_0,\psi_0)$ are certified to a
box of width $8\cdot10^{-10}$ in $q_0$ and $1.2\cdot10^{-8}$ in
$\psi_0$, so the moment point $a^\star$ can be
stored as decimals and every enclosure treats them as exact; at
$\kappa=0.05$ the hull of the boxes located at the two ends of the
deep $\alpha_\star$ interval has width $1.5\cdot10^{-6}$ in $q_0$ and
$9.2\cdot10^{-5}$ in $\psi_0$ (at each end $q_0$ is located to
$1.5\cdot10^{-9}$ and $\psi_0$ is enclosed as $R(q_0,\alpha)$ by a
Riemann sum of $4000$ cells, to width $6.9\cdot10^{-5}$).  The
certificates of this section use this hull; the box of
Section~\ref{sec:hessian} is narrower in $\psi_0$ and wider in $q_0$,
and both contain the fixed point for every $\alpha$ of the deep
$\alpha_\star$ interval.  The width of the hull enters every plain evaluation of
$\Phi$ at about $2\cdot10^{-5}$, much wider than the differences of
order $10^{-7}$ whose signs place the dual points.  Four changes make the four certificates apply at
$\kappa\ne0$: $a^\star$ computed from the box, one tilt slope per
angular sector on the rays, differences of $\Phi$ written against
$b_0=(1,0)$, and kernels that stay finite where the argument of the
inverse Mills ratio is large.

\emph{The moment point from the box.}  Every certificate that references
$a^\star$ or the optimal tilt $s_0$ uses them as the functions
$(\,\psi_0(1-q_0),\,q_0\,)$ and $\sqrt{1-q_0}$ of the box, never as
stored decimals; the enclosure of $a^\star$ is the image of the box
under the first of these maps.  The true $a^\star$, at the true
parameters, is therefore covered exactly, and the identities at
$a^\star$ ($\sS=0$, $\nabla\sS=0$ and tilt optimality, at the true
$a^\star$) hold on the certified rays with no enlargement of the
enclosure.  Certificates evaluated over the enclosure of $a^\star$
cover in particular the rays from the true $a^\star$.

\emph{The ray argument.}  The ray part
of~\cite[Lemma~6.1]{shmalo2026capacity} carries over; what changes at
$\kappa\ne0$ is the choice of slope.  For a unit direction $v$ and a slope
$c\in\R$ put $\chi_{v,c}(t)=\mathcal F(a^\star+tv,\,s_0+ct)$, with
$\mathcal F$ as in Section~\ref{sec:twovarfn}, so that
$\bar\sS(a^\star+tv)\le\chi_{v,c}(t)$ whenever $s_0+ct\ge0$.  At
the true parameters $\chi_{v,c}(0)=\chi_{v,c}'(0)=0$ for every $c$,
by the identities at $a^\star$.  If
$\chi_{v,c}''\le0$ on $[0,T(v)]$ then $\chi_{v,c}\le0$ there, hence
$\bar\sS\le0$ on the segment.  The chain needs one slope per ray: the
second-derivative bound on a radial band $[t_0,t_1]$ with $t_0>0$
says nothing about $\chi$ itself unless $\chi$ and $\chi'$ are
controlled at $t_0$, and those are controlled only through the same
function on $[0,t_0]$.  The certificates therefore fix the slope by
direction alone: the circle is cut into $768$ equal sectors, each
sector carries one slope (the optimal tilt slope at its midpoint,
rounded to six decimals), every angular
cell of every radial band lies inside one sector and uses that
sector's slope, and a direction on a sector edge is covered by the
cells of either neighbor, each with its own fixed slope.  Each cell
lies in one sector, the cells of each band tile its angle range, and
the main and supplementary certificates use the same slope table.

\emph{Differences against $b_0$.}  The dual localization
of~\cite[Lemma~6.1]{shmalo2026capacity}, which keeps the dual maximizer
$\lambda(x)$ inside a certified region of the dual plane (its dual
enclosure) so that the entropy Hessian can be
bounded there, needs signs of differences
$\Phi(\lambda)-\Phi(\lambda')-(\lambda-\lambda')\cdot x$ for pairs of
dual points $\lambda,\lambda'$ and moment points $x$ near $a^\star$.
Evaluated as differences of plain $\Phi$ enclosures these carry the
width of the box twice.  Written
against $b_0=(1,0)$, the integrand collapses exactly:
with $\Delta=(\lambda_1-1)\dot H+\lambda_2M$,
\[
\log\frac{\cosh(\dot H+\Delta)}{\cosh\dot H}
-(\lambda-b_0)\cdot\nabla_b\log2\cosh(b\cdot(\dot H,M))\bigr|_{b_0}
=\log\bigl(\cosh\Delta+M\sinh\Delta\bigr)-M\Delta ,
\]
because the gradient of $b\mapsto\log2\cosh(b\cdot(\dot H,M))$ at
$b_0$ is $M\,(\dot H,M)$, whose inner product with $\lambda-b_0$ is
$M\Delta$.  Every
factor is a small ball with thin coefficients, so the parameter
width enters only at second order in $|\lambda-b_0|$; within
distance $0.01$ of $b_0$, where plain differences of enclosures fail,
the enclosures are $50$ to $160$ times tighter, and at $b_0$ the enclosure is
exactly $\{0\}$.  Differences between arbitrary points are then
differences of two such enclosures, and the gradient term the
localization needs is the same trick applied to $\nabla\Phi$:
$\nabla\Phi(\lambda)-\nabla\Phi(b_0)$ has the exact per-node form
$(\dot H,M)\,(1-M^2)\tanh\Delta/(1+M\tanh\Delta)$.

The factor $\cosh\Delta+M\sinh\Delta$ itself has two exact
evaluations with disjoint stability regimes: the cosh/sinh form is
exact at $\Delta=0$ (which the differences of order $10^{-7}$ in the bands nearest
$a^\star$ require) but subtracts two $e^{|\Delta|}$-sized balls when
$M\approx\pm1$ and $|\Delta|$ is large, where the enclosure of the
factor contains $0$ and its logarithm is not enclosed; the
split $e^{\Delta}(1+M)/2+e^{-\Delta}(1-M)/2$ has both terms
nonnegative on the real axis but carries first-order $M$-width near
$\Delta=0$.  The first form is used at nodes with $|\Delta|\le1$,
the second at the others; both forms are identities, so the choice
affects tightness only.

\emph{Kernels at large arguments.}  The ray certificates use second
derivatives of the constraint term, whose kernels involve
$E'(V)=E(V)(E(V)-V)$ and $E''(V)$, with $E$ the inverse Mills ratio
of Section~\ref{sec:system}, evaluated at arguments $V$ that reach
about $20$ on far quadrature cells; there $E(V)-V$ is a difference of
two large balls and the enclosures of the kernels are useless.  The bounds of the following lemma, applied
as intersections with the computed balls, remove the failure; the
intersection of an enclosure with a proven bound is again an
enclosure.

\begin{lemma}[bounds on the inverse Mills ratio]\label{lem:mills}
Let $E$ be the inverse Mills ratio and $\sigma=E-V$.  For every
$V\in\R$,
\[
\sigma>0\ \ \Bigl(\text{and } \sigma<\frac1V \text{ for } V>0\Bigr),\qquad
E'=E\sigma\in(0,1),\qquad
0<E''<\sigma .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $V\le0$, $\sigma>0$ because $E>0$.  For $V>0$ the bounds
$V<E(V)<V+1/V$ are Gordon's inequalities~\cite{gordon1941}, which give
$0<\sigma<1/V$.  The bounds $0<E'<1$ are due to
Sampford~\cite{sampford1953}; in the present notation $E'=E^2-VE=E\sigma$,
and $1-E'=\operatorname{Var}(Z\mid Z>V)$ by the conditional moments
$\E[Z\mid Z>V]=E$ and $\E[Z^2\mid Z>V]=1+VE$, which are used again
below.  The upper bound on $E''$ is
the identity $E''-\sigma=(E'-1)(E+\sigma)$ with $E'<1$ and $E+\sigma>0$.

Positivity of $E''$ is the convexity of the inverse Mills ratio.
Differentiating $E'$ gives
$E''=E\,[\,\sigma(E+\sigma)-1\,]$, and for $W$ distributed as $Z-V$ conditioned
on $\{Z>V\}$ the moment formulas above read $\E W=\sigma$ and
$\E[W^2]=1-VE+V^2$, whence $\sigma(E+\sigma)-1=2(\E W)^2-\E[W^2]$: the claim is
that this residual law has coefficient of variation below one.  Its
survival function is
$\Prob(W>w)=\Psi(V+w)/\Psi(V)=e^{-\Gamma(w)}$ with
$\Gamma(w)=\int_0^w E(V+x)\,dx$ convex, since $E'>0$.  Set $\theta=1/\sigma$,
so the rate-$\theta$ exponential has the same mean, and
$J(w)=\Gamma(w)-\theta w$.  Then $J$ is convex with $J(0)=0$,
$J'(0)=E-1/\sigma<0$ (this is $E\sigma=E'<1$ again), and $J(w)\to\infty$, so
$J<0$ exactly on some interval $(0,w_0)$ and $J>0$ past it.  Hence
$\Prob(W>w)-e^{-\theta w}=e^{-\theta w}(e^{-J(w)}-1)$ is positive
before $w_0$ and negative after, while
$\int_0^\infty(\Prob(W>w)-e^{-\theta w})\,dw=\sigma-\sigma=0$.  Therefore
\[
\tfrac12\,\E[W^2]-\sigma^2
=\int_0^\infty w\,\bigl(\Prob(W>w)-e^{-\theta w}\bigr)\,dw
=\int_0^\infty (w-w_0)\bigl(\Prob(W>w)-e^{-\theta w}\bigr)\,dw<0,
\]
the last integrand being nonpositive and not almost everywhere zero.
So $\E[W^2]<2(\E W)^2$, that is $E''>0$, at every real $V$.
\end{proof}

With these four changes
the four certificates hold at each of the five
margins.  We give the counts at $\kappa=0.05$; the other margins are in
Section~\ref{sec:margins}.  At $\kappa=0.05$, on
that hull and the deep $\alpha_\star$ interval of
Section~\ref{sec:slabs}:

\item[(i)] \emph{Region II}: the $1296$ cells of a $48\times27$ grid

\item[(ii)] \emph{Region I}: the ray certificates

four of Huang's conditions hold there, and his theorem gives the
unconditional bound.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $\kappa\in\{-0.45,\,-0.05,\,0.05,\,0.0995,\,0.13\}$.  Then
$\alpha_\star(\kappa)$ lies in the corresponding interval
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\alpha_\star(-0.45)\in[1.557652103,\,1.557654133], &
\alpha_\star(-0.05)\in[0.889408031,\,0.889409783],\\
\alpha_\star(0.05)\in[0.781073068,\,0.781074776], &
\alpha_\star(0.0995)\in[0.733478513,\,0.733480205],\\
\alpha_\star(0.13)\in[0.705932217,\,0.705933898], &
\end{array}
\]
and, by \cite[Theorem~3.6]{huang2024capacity}, for every $\alpha$ above that interval
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\Prob\Bigl(\exists\,w\in\{-1,+1\}^N:\
\frac{\langle g_a,w\rangle}{\sqrt N}\ge\kappa\ \text{for all }
a\le\lceil\alpha N\rceil\Bigr)=0 .
\]
\end{theorem}

conditions hold on the whole strip.  The $1680$ slabs, each with

\section{What is checked in exact arithmetic}\label{sec:metaverify}

Every inequality of Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and~\ref{thm:strip} is
decided by a comparison of arb balls (python-flint 0.9.0 on FLINT
3.6.0), built from the primitives of \cite{shmalo2026capacity}:
monotone endpoint evaluations, mean-value cells, correlated
integrands and tail bounds.  The identities the certificates use are
Lemmas~\ref{lem:stat}, \ref{lem:dR} and~\ref{lem:mills}, the formula
for $\ell$ (Section~\ref{sec:architecture}), the constraint constants
$C_j$ (Section~\ref{sec:hessian}) and the identity written against
$b_0$ (Section~\ref{sec:regionI}).

The certificates of Condition~\ref{cond:2var} record every
sign-critical quantity as an outward-rounded decimal in canonical
JSON, bound by SHA-256 hashes; the stage-2 file binds the hash of the
Region~I file it reads.  Their exact layer is restated over the
integers and checked by the Lean~4 kernel (core library, v4.35.0):
the inequality chain of the star interior and the positivity of its
perturbation determinant, the positive definiteness of the Loewner
majorants $\bar B$ at all five margins, and the gap-free tilings of the
Region~II and stage-2 grids and of the $1404$-band Region~I
schedule.  Two global clauses on the band geometry, that the
innermost ring closes the full circle and that each level's angular
support lies inside the previous level's, together make every radial
chain contiguous out to the star radius.  These two clauses and the
analytic lemmas are not part of the statement checked in Lean.

The enclosures behind Theorem~\ref{thm:strip} are not restated in
exact arithmetic; that theorem rests on the ball-arithmetic
computation alone.

\section{What the curve says about learning}\label{sec:ml}

For binary weights the Euclidean norm is fixed at $\sqrt N$, so a
margin constraint is a robustness certificate: if
$y_a\langle w,x_a\rangle/\sqrt N\ge\kappa$, the label $y_a$ is
unchanged under every perturbation of $x_a$ of Euclidean norm below
$\kappa$.  With standard Gaussian designs and independent
labels, folding the labels into the design makes the event that a
binary $w$ stores every label with robustness radius $\kappa$ the
satisfiability event studied here.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} at
$\kappa=0.05$ therefore says that for at least $0.781075N$ random
labels, with probability tending to one as $N\to\infty$, no binary
weight vector stores all of them with robustness radius $0.05$.

On the certified strip the logarithm of $\alpha_\star$ decreases at a
rate that falls from about $1.55$ per unit margin at $\kappa=-0.65$
to about $1.26$ near $\kappa=0.1$ ($1.40$ on average), so the
capacity halves for every $0.45$ to $0.55$ of demanded radius.  In
floating point, the ratio $\alpha_\star/\alpha_{\mathrm{sph}}$ grows
from $0.417$ at $\kappa=0$ to $0.556$ at $\kappa=1.5$: against
real-valued weights, binary weights give up proportionally less
storage as the demanded radius grows.  The radius is measured in
feature space; a statement in input space needs a Lipschitz bound on
the feature map.

\section{Scope and further directions}\label{sec:scope}

\emph{Other margins of the same family.}  The certificates of
Section~\ref{sec:regionI} are run here at five margins in
$[-0.45,0.13]$.  Along the continuation $\mathcal H$ is negative
definite up to $\kappa=1.15$ and loses definiteness near
$\kappa\approx1.18$, beyond which a local argument must re-optimize
the tilt (Section~\ref{sec:hessian}); on the negative side the
continuation is computed down to $\kappa=-2$.  Toward deep negative margin the weak
eigenvalue of $\mathcal H$ and the slack in $\ell_0$ shrink; whether
Condition~\ref{cond:2var} holds at every negative margin is open.

\emph{The lower bound.}  Ding and Sun's conditioned second-moment
argument is carried out at $\kappa=0$; its hypothesis at general
margin is the one-variable inequality $S_{\kappa,\alpha_\star}<0$ of
Section~\ref{sec:lower}, which holds in floating point at the five
margins.
